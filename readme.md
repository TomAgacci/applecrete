APPLE PULP • HYDRATED LIME • SAND • PARALLEL SALT BLEND

The crete is slated to be able to support 4mpa blocks.

Notes Making the Crete and Apple Preparation

======================================================
I - Making The Apple Juice and The Pulp
======================================================


RAW APPLE JUICE — NO HEAT, NO WATER
Equipment:
- Blender
- Coffee filters (paper)
- Bowl, jar, or funnel
- Clean apples (any variety)

Procedure:
1. Wash apples thoroughly. Remove stems and any damaged spots.
2. Cut apples into chunks. Remove seeds if desired.
3. Load apple chunks into blender. Do NOT add water.
4. Blend on high until the mixture becomes a smooth apple puree.
5. Place a coffee filter inside a funnel or over a jar/bowl.
6. Pour the apple puree into the coffee filter.
7. Allow gravity to drip the raw apple juice through the filter.
8. Do NOT squeeze the filter — squeezing forces pulp through.
9. Wait 10–30 minutes for full drip-through.
10. For clearer juice, run the liquid through a second coffee filter.
11. Refrigerate immediately. Consume within 24–48 hours.

Output:
- Cold-processed raw apple juice
- Crisp, bright flavor
- Naturally cloudy unless double-filtered

======================================================
II - Making the Apple Crete From The Pulp
======================================================

======================================================
1. TARGET & GENERAL NOTES
======================================================
TARGET:
- Compressive strength: ~4 MPa at 28 days.
- Application: non-structural blocks/panels, insulation, experimental biocomposite.

BINDER SYSTEM:
- Lime-based (hydrated lime, air-hardening via carbonation).
- No Portland cement in this version.

AGGREGATE SYSTEM:
- Fine mineral aggregate (sand) for strength.
- Apple pulp as lightweight bio-aggregate.

ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS (IDEAL):
- Ambient temperature: 18–25 °C.
- Relative humidity: 40–70%.
- No direct sun, no rain exposure during early cure.

======================================================
2. MATERIALS & EXAMPLE BATCH
======================================================
BASE RATIOS (BY MASS):
- Fine sand (0–2 mm):          4.0 parts
- Hydrated lime (builder’s):   2.0 parts
- Apple pulp (dried):          1.0 part
- Parallel salt blend:         0.15 parts
- Water:                       1.5–2.0 parts (tuned to workability)

EXAMPLE 10 kg BATCH (DRY MATERIALS ONLY):
Let “1 part” = 1 kg for clarity.

- Sand:        4.0 kg
- Lime:        2.0 kg
- Apple pulp:  1.0 kg (dried granules)
- Salt blend:  0.15 kg (150 g)
- Water:       Start at 1.5 kg, adjust up to ~2.0 kg as needed.

PARALLEL SALT BLEND COMPOSITION (BY MASS):
- NaCl (non-iodized):          2 parts
- CaCl₂ (flake/pellet):        1 part
- NaHCO₃ (baking soda):        1 part

Example for 150 g salt blend:
- NaCl:   75 g
- CaCl₂:  37.5 g
- NaHCO₃: 37.5 g

======================================================
3. APPLE PULP PREPARATION
======================================================
SOURCE:
- Use the solid residue from your raw apple juice filtration (coffee filters).

STEPS:
1. COLLECTION
   - Scrape pulp from filters into a tray.
   - Remove any visible paper fibers or foreign material.

2. DRYING
   - Spread pulp in a thin layer (5–10 mm) on a non-reactive tray (plastic or stainless).
   - Air-dry 12–24 hours at room temperature.
   - Optionally use a fan for airflow, but avoid heated drying above ~40 °C.

3. MOISTURE TARGET
   - Pulp should:
     - Feel dry on the surface.
     - Not release free water when squeezed firmly.
     - Still have some internal flexibility (not rock-hard).

4. GRANULATION
   - Break dried pulp into granules ~2–8 mm.
   - Avoid powdering; you want discrete particles that behave like aggregate.
   - Remove any oversized clumps >10 mm.

5. STORAGE
   - Store in a breathable container (paper bag or mesh) until use.
   - Avoid sealed wet storage (mold risk).

======================================================
4. PARALLEL SALT BLEND PREPARATION
======================================================
STEPS:
1. DRY MIX
   - In a dry container, combine:
     - 2 parts NaCl
     - 1 part CaCl₂
     - 1 part NaHCO₃
   - Break up any lumps, especially in CaCl₂ and NaHCO₃.

2. HOMOGENIZATION
   - Stir or shake until visually uniform.
   - Label container with composition and date.

3. STORAGE
   - Keep sealed and dry.
   - Hygroscopic behavior (especially CaCl₂) will pull moisture if left open.

======================================================
5. DRY MIXING PROCEDURE
======================================================
EQUIPMENT:
- Mixing tub or mortar mixer.
- Scoop or trowel.
- Scale for weighing.

STEPS:
1. WEIGH MATERIALS
   - Sand: 4.0 kg
   - Lime: 2.0 kg
   - Salt blend: 0.15 kg

2. INITIAL BLEND
   - Add sand and lime to the mixing tub.
   - Dry-mix for 2–3 minutes until color is uniform.

3. ADD SALT BLEND
   - Sprinkle salt blend evenly over the surface.
   - Mix another 2–3 minutes.
   - Goal: no visible pockets of salt or lime.

======================================================
6. ADDING APPLE PULP
======================================================
STEPS:
1. ADD PULP
   - Add 1.0 kg of prepared apple pulp granules to the dry mix.

2. COATING
   - Fold and toss the mix so pulp is fully coated with lime/sand/salt.
   - Check by grabbing a handful:
     - Pulp particles should be individually coated, not clumped.

3. VISUAL CHECK
   - Mix should look like a light aggregate concrete:
     - Mineral matrix with visible organic inclusions.
     - No large uncoated pulp clusters.

======================================================
7. HYDRATION & WORKABILITY
======================================================
STEPS:
1. INITIAL WATER ADDITION
   - Add ~1.5 kg water gradually while mixing.
   - Do NOT dump all water at once; add in 3–4 increments.

2. WORKABILITY TARGET
   - After each addition, mix 1–2 minutes.
   - Desired consistency:
     - Stiff, cohesive.
     - Holds shape when squeezed in a gloved hand.
     - Slight surface sheen.
     - No free water pooling at the bottom of the tub.

3. FINE TUNING
   - If mix is too dry and crumbly:
     - Add water in 100–200 g increments.
   - If mix becomes soupy:
     - Add small amounts of sand and lime (maintain 2:4 ratio) to restore stiffness.

4. RECORDING
   - Record final water mass used.
   - Note ambient temperature and humidity for future comparison.

======================================================
8. CASTING INTO MOLDS
======================================================
MOLD TYPE:
- 50 mm cubes or cylinders (e.g., 50×100 mm) for compressive tests.
- Larger blocks/panels for practical evaluation.

STEPS:
1. MOLD PREP
   - Clean molds.
   - Lightly oil or use release agent compatible with lime-based mixes.

2. PLACEMENT
   - Fill molds in 2–3 lifts.
   - For each lift:
     - Place material.
     - Lightly tamp with a rod or trowel to remove large voids.
   - Avoid heavy vibration or over-compaction:
     - You want some internal porosity for insulation and proper pulp distribution.

3. SURFACE FINISH
   - Strike off the top flush with mold edges.
   - Optionally trowel smooth or leave slightly textured.

======================================================
9. CURING REGIME (CRITICAL FOR STRENGTH)
======================================================
PHASE 1 — INITIAL SET (0–24 HOURS):
- Keep molds in a shaded, ventilated area.
- Cover loosely with plastic or damp cloth to prevent rapid surface drying.
- Do not expose to direct sun or wind.

PHASE 2 — EARLY CURE (DAY 1–7):
- Demold after 24–48 hours if the material is firm enough.
- Place specimens on racks, spaced to allow airflow.
- Maintain moderate humidity:
  - Nearby water trays or light misting of surrounding air (not direct soaking).
- Goal: avoid cracking from rapid drying while allowing lime to begin carbonation.

PHASE 3 — MAIN CURE (DAY 7–28):
- Store in a dry, ventilated environment.
- Avoid immersion or heavy wetting:
  - Lime binder relies on CO₂ from air for carbonation.
- Rotate specimens occasionally to ensure even exposure.

NOTES:
- Temperature swings and very low humidity can reduce strength.
- High humidity with poor ventilation can increase mold risk on pulp.

======================================================
10. TESTING & ADJUSTMENT FOR 4 MPa
======================================================
TESTING:
- At 7 days:
  - Perform preliminary compressive tests (expect <4 MPa).
- At 28 days:
  - Perform main compressive tests.
  - Record:
    - Failure load.
    - Failure mode (brittle, ductile, shear).
    - Density (mass/volume).

ADJUSTMENT RULES:
- If strength <4 MPa:
  - Increase mineral fraction:
    - Sand: 4.5 parts
    - Pulp: 0.8 parts
    - Lime: keep at 2.0 parts
  - Keep salt blend at 0.15 parts.
  - Repeat batch and compare.

- If mix is too brittle (snaps easily, low toughness):
  - Slightly increase pulp:
    - Pulp: 1.2 parts
    - Sand: 3.8 parts
  - Keep lime at 2.0 parts.

- If excessive efflorescence (white salt crusts):
  - Reduce salt blend to 0.10 parts.
  - Maintain other ratios.

======================================================
11. SAFETY & LONG-TERM BEHAVIOR
======================================================
SAFETY:
- Wear gloves, mask, and eye protection when handling lime, sand, and salts.
- Avoid inhaling dust.
- Wash skin after contact with wet lime mixes (alkaline).

LONG-TERM BEHAVIOR:
- Organic pulp may slowly degrade:
  - Risk of mold in high humidity.
  - Possible odor if used in enclosed spaces without sealing.
- Recommended:
  - Use as infill or non-critical panels.
  - Apply breathable sealers or coatings if used indoors.

STRUCTURAL NOTE:
- Do NOT use for load-bearing structural elements.
- Treat as experimental biocomposite until fully characterized.
